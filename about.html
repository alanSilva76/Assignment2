The History

On the morning of September 16, 1810, a Catholic priest named Father Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla rang his church bell in the small town of Dolores

In Mexico, September is celebrated as the Mexican Patriotic Month, a time to honor the country's history, culture, and independence. The month is marked by various festivities, including parades, traditional music and dance performances, and the display of the Mexican flag. It is a period of national pride and reflection on the values that define the nation.

Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla was a Mexican Roman Catholic priest and key figure in the Mexican War of Independence (1810–21). Hidalgo is best remembered for his speech, the “Grito de Dolores” (“Cry of Dolores”), which called for the end of Spanish colonial rule in Mexico. Today, Hidalgo is celebrated as “the father of Mexican independence.”

After the French invasion of Spain in 1808, many Mexicans formed secret societies—some supporting the Spanish government, others advocating independence from Spain. Hidalgo joined a pro-independence group near Dolores, Mexico. On September 16, 1810—the date now celebrated as Mexican Independence Day—Hidalgo issued the “Grito de Dolores” (“Cry of Dolores”), calling for the end of Spanish rule, for racial equality, and for redistribution of land. The speech effectively launched the Mexican War of Independence (1810–21).

Why This Date Matters

Even though actual independence from Spain was not fully won until 1821 (after an 11-year war), Mexicans choose to celebrate the spark of the revolution rather than the final treaty. Father Hidalgo's speech united the people and began the long journey toward a sovereign nation.

How It Is Celebrated

Celebrations actually begin on the night of September 15.

Late on the 15th, community leaders and the president recreate the famous Grito, shouting out cheers of "¡Viva México!" to crowds before midnight.

The official holiday on September 16 is filled with vibrant parades, traditional music, food, and fireworks.
